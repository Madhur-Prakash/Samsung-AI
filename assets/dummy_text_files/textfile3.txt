Insertion sort builds the sorted list one element at a time by inserting each new element into its correct position. This makes it very efficient for small datasets or lists that are already nearly sorted. Its best-case time complexity is O(n) which occurs when the list is almost sorted. The worst case is O(n²) when the list is in reverse order. Insertion sort is stable and in-place. It mimics the way people naturally sort playing cards in their hands. Due to its low overhead it is used as a base case in advanced algorithms like merge sort or Timsort.
